## 2. The Problem

### 2.1 The Reproducibility Crisis

Academia is strugling with a reproducibility crisis.  Published estimates of
irreproducibility in the life sciences range from 51% [@hartshorne2012tracking]
to 89% [@begley2012raise] and it is estimated that irreproducibility wastes
approximately 28 billion dollars a year in the US alone [@freedman2015economics].

Many of the systemic causes of irreproducibility can ultimately be traced to an
underlying lack of a standards and best practices framework [@global2013case] or
the lack of data managment infrastrcture and quality control
[@soundararajan2023data].  Additionaly, while academia has peer review for
*results*, it rarely peer reviews *process*.

### 2.2 The Translation Gap

Furthermore, translating validated and reproducible results into the clinic is
a daunting and difficult task, known as "the valley of death"
[@coller2009traversing].

Academic institutions lack access to the quality systems needed to produce
regulated products, or evidence supporting them, in a regulatory-compliant
manner [@coller2009traversing].

Data are assessed for relevance and reliability before they can inform a
regulatory decision [@fda2023rwe]. Consequently, data originating in academia
which has not been generated under prospectivly defined and documented
procedures cannot serve as the primary basis for a determination of substantial
evidence.  At best it can provide corroborative support, and only where it is
"carefully conducted and documented". Reports lacking the details that permit
scientific evaluation are not considered at all [@cfr314.126e].
